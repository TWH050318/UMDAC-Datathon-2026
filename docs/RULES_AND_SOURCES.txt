RECONSTRUCTED RULES, WITH CHART AND CODE SOURCES

Instructions / How to read this file
- Each rule = A relational statement that can be added directly to a dashboard (written in English for easy copy-pasting).
- STATUS: PROVEN = Valid for 100% of the full dataset; SUPPORTED = Backed by quantitative evidence, with alternative explanations ruled out;
OPEN = A pattern has been observed, but the underlying mechanism remains undetermined; REJECTED = Proven invalid upon testing (these are also reported).
- CHART = A chart located in `outputs/charts/`; TABLE = An aggregated table in `outputs/csv/` (used as a data source for Power BI); 
CODE = A notebook and specific section heading within `notebooks/`. "(no chart)" indicates that only a table is available.
- All figures are calculated based on the full dataset of 10,000,000 rows, unless a specific scope is stated. Every notebook
terminates at the import stage unless the dataset contains exactly 10,000,000 rows (see `data_issues_log #11`).
- "Unlabelled" = The `ANOMALY_TYPE` field is empty. Since the case study treats labelling as optional,
"unlabelled" payments are simply those with no recorded anomaly—they are not necessarily verified as legitimate.
- HELD-OUT = A rule derived from the first two-thirds of the time period (up to June 28, 2023) and then evaluated
against the final one-third—data the rule had not previously encountered (see `notebooks/06_validation.ipynb`).
- The suggested dashboard page is indicated in the group heading.
- WHY (interpretation) = Our interpretation of "why this occurs in the real world"—this is an inference, not a direct data output.
ALTERNATIVE = An alternative explanation that was considered, along with the reasoning for its exclusion. NOTE = Discrepancies with real-world conditions or additional validation steps. 
Explanations are based solely on general banking knowledge; no external data sources were used.

[R2] TRANSACTION_TYPE_CODE is "Direct" if and only if the path has exactly 2 banks.
     Size/scope: all rows, 0 exceptions.                    STATUS: PROVEN
     CHART: (no chart)   TABLE: (shown in notebook)
     CODE: 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-A1
     WHY (interpretation): A direct payment means the two banks hold an account
       relationship with each other and need no intermediary. The label is a definition
       applied to the path.

[R3] Path length is geometric: each additional bank keeps 36.8% of the payments
     (ratio 0.363-0.372 for 2 to 9 banks), in every customer flow and for domestic
     and cross-border payments alike. 0.368 is close to 1/e; we cannot confirm that
     this is the intended parameter.                        STATUS: SUPPORTED
     CHART: A2_participant_count_geometric.png   TABLE: routing_participant_count.csv
     CODE: 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-A2
     WHY (interpretation): Banks without a direct relationship pass a payment through
       correspondent banks. If, at every step, there is the same chance that one more
       intermediary is needed, path lengths follow exactly this memoryless (geometric)
       law.
     ALTERNATIVE: Longer chains for cross-border than domestic payments (distance drives
       intermediaries). Rejected: the ratio is the same for both.
     NOTE: Real domestic payments rarely travel through chains of correspondents, so
       applying one ratio everywhere looks like a simplification of the generator.

[R4] With 3 or more banks, a payment is "Covered" with a fixed probability that does
     not depend on path length or amount: 20% if domestic, 10.6% if cross-border
     (otherwise "Indirect").                                STATUS: SUPPORTED
     CHART: E10_covered_share.png   TABLE: routing_covered_by_k_domestic.csv, routing_covered_share.csv
     CODE: 05_completion.ipynb, section E; 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-A3
     WHY (interpretation): In a cover payment the customer instruction goes straight to
       the beneficiary's bank while the funds settle separately through correspondents.
       Twice the share for domestic payments fits banks of one country messaging each
       other directly and settling through a shared correspondent.
     ALTERNATIVE: Cover is used for large amounts. Rejected: the share is flat across
       amounts.

[R7] Each end of the path is replaced by a random bank in 5% of payments,
     independently (first 4.98%, last 4.99%, both 0.247% vs 0.249% expected).
     ORDERING_BIC8 / BENEFICIARY_BIC8 keep the customer's real bank: in these rows the
     IBAN country and bank code still match the BIC column in 100% of cases, while the
     path's end bank is in the same country only 4.8% of the time. Amount, duration and
     routing are unchanged. Labelled anomalies are about half as frequent in these rows
     (3.9 vs 7.3 per 10,000) - not explained.               STATUS: SUPPORTED (meaning OPEN)
     CHART: (no chart)   TABLE: endpoint_mismatch.csv
     CODE: 05_completion.ipynb, section C; 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-A4
     WHY (interpretation): Our reading: the bank that holds the customer's account is
       not always the bank that sends or receives the message - smaller banks reach the
       network through another institution, and groups route through a central entity.
     ALTERNATIVE: A data error. Unlikely: the rate is exactly 5% at each end and the two
       ends are independent, which looks designed.
     NOTE: This is the least certain interpretation in the file; the replacement bank is
       usually in another country, which a sponsor-bank story does not fully explain.

[R5] CONTINUITY_QFLAG = "N" if and only if BIC8_PATH contains "...".
     Size/scope: all rows, 0 exceptions.                    STATUS: PROVEN

[R6] Each link of a path is hidden independently with probability 5%, so the share of
     discontinuous paths is 1 - 0.95^(k-1) for k banks (observed 5.0%, 9.7%, 14.2%,
     18.5%, 22.5% for k = 2..6; model within 0.1 point).    STATUS: SUPPORTED
     CHART: B1_gap_model.png   TABLE: continuity_gap_model.csv
     CODE: 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-B1
     WHY (interpretation): Not every leg of a payment is visible to the network: a bank
       may not report, or a leg may be settled outside the tracked channel. If each leg
       is missed independently, longer paths are proportionally more likely to show a
       gap - which is what the data does.

[D1] Duration is decided by the banks on the path, not by the payment. HELD-OUT: the
     average duration of each bank pair, learned on earlier direct payments, explains
     98.20% of the variance of log10(duration) of later direct payments and predicts
     96.59% of them within a factor of 2.
     COVERAGE: these figures apply only to test payments whose bank pair had 5+ training
     payments = 1,536,936 payments, 76.7% of the eligible test payments (direct payments
     with a visible path in the test period). The other 23.3% belong to rare bank pairs
     and are not scored. A random half/half split inside each pair gives 98.17%.
     Customer type, weekend, hour and amount add almost nothing. (In-sample: 98.4%.)
                                                            STATUS: SUPPORTED
     CHART: B2_duration_direct_bimodal.png, F1_validation_summary.png
     TABLE: validation_duration_pair_heldout.csv, duration_variance_explained.csv
     CODE: 06_validation.ipynb, section 1; 04_open_questions.ipynb, section 1 (H1)
     WHY (interpretation): Processing time is an operational property of a bank:
       automated straight-through processing takes seconds, manual handling and batch
       runs take minutes to an hour. A payment inherits the speed of the banks that
       touch it.
     ALTERNATIVE: Delays come from business hours and cut-off times, or from compliance
       checks on large amounts. Rejected: the slow share barely changes with hour,
       weekend or amount.
     NOTE: In reality cut-off times and weekends do delay payments; their absence here
       is a simplification.

[D2] Each bank has its own processing delay and delays add up along the path. HELD-OUT:
     one delay per bank, fitted on earlier 2-bank payments, predicts later 3-bank Indirect
     payments with log correlation r = 0.932, but only 60.73% within a factor of 2
     (Covered: r = 0.83 and 49.95%; n = 619,906 and 78,698). The accuracy depends on the banks: paths with a slow bank are predicted
     within a factor of 2 in 100% of cases (184k payments), paths of fast banks only in
     34% (the model over-predicts them about 2x). Median duration rises 39 s -> 126 ->
     418 -> 590 -> 796 s for 2..6 banks.
                                                            STATUS: SUPPORTED for slow banks; fast banks and Covered OPEN
     CHART: D1_duration_additive_validation.png, B2_duration_by_participants.png, F1_validation_summary.png
     TABLE: validation_duration_additive_heldout.csv, duration_by_participants.csv
     CODE: 06_validation.ipynb, section 2; 04_open_questions.ipynb, section 1 (H2)
     WHY (interpretation): Banks handle a payment one after another, so their processing
       times accumulate along the chain.
     NOTE: Check: this predicts that a given pair of banks is consistently fast or
       consistently slow, whatever the payment - observed (D1).

[D3] Large banks are fast: among banks with 300+ direct payments, only 0.5% of the
     largest eighth are slow (delay over 150 s) against 50-55% in every other size group.
                                                            STATUS: SUPPORTED
     CHART: D1_bank_delay_distribution.png   TABLE: bank_delay_by_size.csv
     CODE: 04_open_questions.ipynb, section 1 (H3)
     WHY (interpretation): Large banks handle the volume that justifies investment in
       real-time processing, and correspondent business depends on speed; small banks
       more often process manually or in batches.

[R8] A link converts currency exactly when it crosses a currency border. Direct
     payments: all 1,608 large country pairs convert either never or always; same
     currency area -> 0 conversions in 927,261 of 927,261 payments; different area ->
     1 conversion in 99.7%. For paths of 3-6 banks, CURRENCY_CONVERSION_COUNT equals
     the number of currency-border crossings in 99.5%.
     HELD-OUT (answers the circularity concern): currency areas learned only from earlier
     direct payments; on later payments the rule is right for 100.00% of direct payments
     between country pairs seen in training (0 exceptions in 1,893,335), 99.52% between
     pairs NEVER seen in training (521 exceptions in 109,519), and FX count = area
     crossings in 99.94% of 3-6-bank paths (1,058,384), which were never used for learning.
                                                            STATUS: SUPPORTED (near-deterministic)
     CHART: E8_fx_rule.png, F1_validation_summary.png
     TABLE: validation_fx_direct_heldout.csv, validation_fx_paths_heldout.csv, fx_rule_summary.csv
     CODE: 06_validation.ipynb, section 3; 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-C2 and H-C3
     Note: the chart's middle bar is defined by the pair never converting; the evidence
     is that no pair lies in between.
     WHY (interpretation): A conversion is needed only when a payment moves between
       banks that keep their accounts in different currencies. Inside a monetary union,
       or between countries using the same currency, nothing has to be exchanged.
     ALTERNATIVE: Conversion depends on the payment currency versus the receiver's
       currency (one conversion per payment). Rejected: the count follows the path, link
       by link.
     NOTE: Real payments are usually converted once; several conversions along one chain
       look like a generator simplification.

     CHART: (no chart)   TABLE: (printed in notebook)
     CODE: 06_validation.ipynb, section 3; 01_routing_duration_fx.ipynb, H-C2
